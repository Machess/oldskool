Why we make games

Screen time should leave a child better off.

Children are going to play on screens. Too much of what is waiting for them there is built to hook them, hype them up or numb them. We started Oldschool to make the opposite: games that ask a child to think, teach something that lasts, and send them back to the real world in a good frame of mind.

Non-profit Free for every family

We don't make money from this.

Oldschool is a non-profit project. The games are free, and they will stay free. We sell nothing, show no ads and take no money from children's attention.

We make them for the families who need them: parents looking for an alternative to the addictive, dopamine-chasing apps that fill most children's screens.

How we build every game

STEP 01

The lesson comes first

We decide what a child should learn, then build the adventure around it. The learning is the gameplay, never a quiz bolted onto something else.

STEP 02

Progress is earned

The only way forward is a right answer and honest effort. Nothing is handed out for showing up, and nothing can be bought.

STEP 03

Stories with a backbone

Every world we write has right and wrong in it. Heroes are honest, hard-working and respectful, and the story shows why that matters.

STEP 04

Parents hold the keys

Every game comes with parental settings. You decide how long a session lasts, and the game itself tells your child when it is time to stop.

Calm by design

High education. Low stimulation.

Most kids' games are tuned to keep thumbs tapping. Ours are tuned to keep minds working. Screen time should be actual learning, never a dopamine drip and never a way to switch a child off.

These are house rules. They hold for every game and every story we will ever release.

Hero stats

A real hero looks after three things.

Alongside the school subjects, our games teach children how to look after themselves. Health in food, movement and mind is written into the stories and the lessons.

Food

In the game, children learn what real food is, where it comes from and what it does for a growing body. Heroes cook and eat proper meals.

Movement

In the game, children learn why strong bodies need fresh air, exercise and plenty of play, and what happens to a hero who sits still too long.

Mind

In the game, children learn about patience, focus, rest and keeping a calm head, and practise all four on problems worth solving.

Parental settings

Then the game says: go and play.

Learning about health only counts if it gets used. Parents set how long their child may play. When the time is up, the game stops at a good place and pops up an invitation to head outside and have a real adventure.

What we stand for

The hero's code.

Every Oldschool hero lives by the same code, whatever world they are in. It is built on the traditional values families have handed down for generations, starting with treating others with respect.

We don't lecture. Children see the code at work in the story and practise it as they play.

  1. Respect others. Parents, teachers, friends and strangers alike.
  2. Tell the truth. Even when it costs you.
  3. Work hard. Nothing worth having is handed out.
  4. Clean up your own mess. And lend a hand with someone else's.
  5. Stand by your family. Home is where the adventure starts.
  6. Say please and thank you. And mean it.
